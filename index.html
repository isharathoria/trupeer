Founding PM assignment

Reducing churn at Trupeer

How I'd find out why customers leave, and what I'd test and build in my first 90 days.

Executive summary

Churn at Trupeer is most likely a repeat-usage problem: customers make one or two videos, get value, and then have no regular reason to come back.

Why not a missing feature?
  • Features are already strong. Trupeer matches or beats competitors on AI voiceover, avatars, translation and auto-generated guides. Simpler tools like Loom and Scribe keep customers without these.
  • New users like the first result. #1 on Product Hunt and good early reviews. The drop comes after the first use, not before it.
  • A missing feature would show up in cancellation reasons. I'd check this in week 1. If it does, the plan changes.
What I'd fix first
1

One-time use. Customers create for a single launch or demo, then stop.

2

Buyer doesn't see the value. One person uses it; the budget owner can't see the impact at renewal.

3

Too much editing. AI output needs fixing, so a video takes far longer than promised.

In parallel: recover failed payments and offer pause or downgrade before someone cancels.

North-star metric

Weekly active accounts whose videos get watched

It shows both that customers keep creating and that their audience uses the content, which is what the buyer pays for.

Goal

Cut monthly customer churn by 25–30% and grow net revenue retention above 110% within two quarters. Final targets get set once we know the baseline.

Days 1–30
Find the cause

Data, customer interviews, failed-payment fixes.

Days 31–60
Test fixes

Small experiments against a control group.

Days 61–90
Build what worked

Ship only what improved retention.

01Defining churnFour numbers, because each has a different cause and a different fix.
MetricWhat it measuresWhy it matters
Customer churn% of paying self-serve customers who cancel each monthTells us whether the product keeps people on its own
Revenue churnRevenue lost each month to cancellations and downgradesA customer can stay but pay less; this catches it
Net revenue retentionRevenue from existing enterprise customers after 12 months, including upgradesShows whether big accounts grow or shrink
Failed-payment churnCustomers lost because a card payment failedNot a real decision to leave, and the easiest to fix

Early warning: new users who never make a second video. They haven't cancelled yet, but most will.

How I'd split every number: by plan, monthly vs annual billing, use case (sales demos, help guides, training), signup source, and how long they've been a customer.

Where I'd start: revenue churn in larger accounts first (most money at risk), then self-serve customer churn (most customers), with failed payments fixed in parallel.

02Finding the causeThree weeks, starting with data that already exists.

From the data

  1. Retention by signup month, split by plan, source and use case. Does usage level off (a loyal core) or keep falling?
  2. Where users drop off: install → first recording → first video shared → second video within 14 days → teammate invited → video watched by others → creating every week.
  3. What loyal customers did early: actions in the first two weeks that customers who stayed took and those who left didn't. For example 3+ videos, brand setup, translation, embedding, or a second user.
  4. Behaviour before cancelling: plan usage, editing time per video, failed exports.
  5. Revenue: downgrades, fewer seats, failed payments and how many get recovered.

From customers

  • Interviews with 30 customers who left and 15 who stayed, both self-serve and enterprise.
  • Cancellation reasons, support tickets and public reviews (G2, Product Hunt).
  • Watch 20 new users' first sessions.
  • Talk to 5 people who watch customers' videos. If nobody watches, the buyer won't renew.
If the data isn't there: add basic tracking in week 1 (recordings, edits, shares, invites, views, cancellation reasons). Pull billing and CRM data, and hold a weekly 30-minute churn review with the founders and customer success.

04What I'd do about itRanked by revenue at risk, strength of evidence and effort. Each fix starts as a small test.
Priority 1 · One-time use

Give customers a reason to come back

  • Update one step of a video when the product changes, instead of re-recording it.
  • Templates for recurring work: release updates, onboarding series, process guides.
  • One shared library where all videos and guides live.
Test: manually update videos for 50 accounts, compare 60-day retention with a control group.
Priority 2 · Buyer doesn't see the value

Show the impact and bring in the team

  • Monthly impact report to the account owner: views, completion rates, top videos, support tickets avoided.
  • Prompt to invite teammates when sharing a video; add comments and approvals.
  • Enterprise: account health tracking and quarterly business reviews.
Test: send the report to half of team accounts, compare renewals and seat growth.
Priority 3 · Too much editing

Get to a good first video faster

  • Guided first video by use case, with a short checklist before recording.
  • Quick rating on voiceover, script and guide steps; fix the most common problems first.
Test: A/B test guided onboarding; measure second video within 14 days and editing time.
Quick wins · Billing and cancellation

Stop avoidable losses

  • Retry failed payments, update expired cards, send reminders.
  • At cancellation, ask why, then offer a pause, a downgrade or extra credits.
  • Test plan limits that match how often people actually create.
Test: add a pause option; measure how many stay and how many return within 90 days.
Not now: more avatars or voices. They make the first video nicer but don't bring anyone back for the second.
05Measuring successOne north star, a few supporting metrics, and warning signs.

North star: weekly active accounts whose videos get watched, meaning they published at least one video that week and someone viewed it.

Supporting metrics

Second video within 14 daysWeeks per month with a new videoActive users per account% of videos shared or embeddedViews per videoEditing time per videoPlan usage
Early warning signs
  • No new video for 3+ weeks
  • Fewer active users or fewer views
  • Hit the plan limit, or barely using it
  • Failed payment or unhappy support tickets
  • Main contact left the company (enterprise)
Early signs trigger an in-app nudge; serious ones trigger a call from customer success.
Guardrails and targets
  • Guardrails: cost per account, support load, customer satisfaction, and "saved" customers who still leave within 90 days.
  • Day 30: baseline and tracking in place.
  • Day 60: 10 points more new users making a second video.
  • Day 90: customer churn down 25–30%; over half of failed payments recovered.

Risks and trade-offs

  • Keeping the wrong customers: if one signup source drives most churn, the fix is marketing, not product.
  • Discounts that only delay churn: track whether "saved" customers leave within 90 days.
  • Video updates are hard to build: do them manually first; build only if retention improves.
  • Small numbers can mislead: back every data point with customer conversations.
  • Flexible limits cost money: check AI costs before changing plans.
What I wouldn't do: add more avatars or voices to fix churn, force unhappy customers into annual contracts, or hide the cancel button.
07Questions for the foundersWhat I'd want to know on day one.
  1. Where is churn highest today: self-serve customers, downgrades, or enterprise renewals?
  2. What share of customers who left made only one or two videos?
  3. Can we see who watches customers' videos, and do loyal customers get more views?
  4. Which signup sources bring the longest-staying customers?
  5. What do customers say when they cancel, and is it recorded anywhere?

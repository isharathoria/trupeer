How I'd diagnose why customers leave, and what I'd build, test and measure in my first 90 days.

Executive summary

An activation and habit problem, not a feature gap

My working view: many accounts come for one demo or launch video and never build a habit around Trupeer.

  • Weeks 1–3Define churn by segment, instrument the creation funnel, and interview 30 churned and 15 retained customers.
  • Test firstOne-off (episodic) use; creator-only adoption where the buyer never sees ROI; AI output that needs more rework than "seconds" promises.
  • BetsMake content living and recurring, make accounts multiplayer, prove ROI to buyers, and fix packaging and failed payments.
  • GoalCut self-serve monthly logo churn by 25–30% (relative) and push enterprise net revenue retention above 110% within two quarters. Illustrative; set after the baseline.
01 · Define

What "churn" means here

I'd track four churn numbers separately, because each has a different cause, owner and fix.

01

Self-serve logo churn

% of paying workspaces that cancel in a month. Health of the product-led motion; most logos sit here.

02

Gross revenue churn

MRR lost to cancels and downgrades. Scale-to-Pro downgrades and seat cuts hide inside "retained" logos.

03

Net revenue retention

Enterprise cohort revenue after 12 months, including expansion. The number investors judge a B2B company on.

04

Involuntary churn

Cancels caused by failed payments that were never recovered. The cheapest churn to fix.

I'd also watch pre-activation drop-off: trial users and new payers who never ship a second piece of content. It isn't churn yet, but it predicts it.

Assumptions, since no data was shared

  • Public pricing: 10-day trial, Pro at $49/month (20 AI video minutes), Scale at $249/month (100 minutes, 3 editors), custom Enterprise. Minutes reset monthly and don't roll over.
  • Most logos are self-serve monthly; most revenue is Scale and Enterprise. So logo churn and revenue churn will tell different stories.
  • Every metric is cut by plan, monthly vs annual, use case (sales demo, support guide, training, release notes, SOPs), acquisition channel and tenure.
  • Order of attack: revenue churn in Scale and Enterprise first, activation-driven logo churn second, involuntary churn in parallel.
02 · Diagnose

How I'd find the cause

Three weeks of diagnosis, starting with data that already exists before adding new tracking.

Quantitative

What the numbers say

  1. Cohort retention curves by signup month, plan, channel and use case. A flattening curve means a healthy core; one that keeps falling means no lasting job for the product.
  2. Creation funnel: install → first recording → first export/share → 2nd piece within 14 days → teammate invited → content viewed → creating weekly.
  3. Retention correlates: first-14-day behaviours that separate retained from churned accounts (3+ pieces, brand kit, translation, embeds, 2+ editors).
  4. Usage before cancel: AI minutes vs limit, edit time per video, regenerations, export failures.
  5. Revenue view: downgrades, seat contraction, failed payments and recovery rate.
Qualitative

What customers say

  • 30 churned and 15 retained customer interviews, split between self-serve and enterprise.
  • Cancel-flow reason codes (added if missing) plus free text.
  • Support tickets by theme; G2 and Product Hunt reviews; sales and CS notes on lost renewals.
  • 20 session recordings of first-time users.
  • 5 conversations with viewers of Trupeer content. If nobody watches the output, the buyer won't renew.
If tracking is thin: ship week-1 events (recording, generation, edit time, share/embed, invite, view, limit hit, cancel reason), pull billing and CRM data, and start a weekly 30-minute churn review with the founders and CS.

04 · Prioritise

What I'd tackle first

Scored on evidence, revenue at risk and effort. My starting ranking:

1

Episodic use

Probably the biggest driver of logo churn. Fixing it builds a moat: a library of living content is hard to leave.

2

Invisible ROI, single-player

Where revenue churn lives, in Scale and Enterprise renewals.

3

AI output needs rework

Breaks the core promise of a finished video in seconds, so it drags down activation everywhere.

In parallel (quick wins): payment-failure recovery, a cancel flow with pause and downgrade options, and annual-plan nudges. Not now: more avatars and voices, or chasing users who were never a fit. If churn turns out to be concentrated in one channel, wrong-fit acquisition jumps to the top.
05 · Solve

How I'd solve it

Each fix is tied to a root cause and starts as a cheap experiment before engineering commits to it.

A · Episodic use

Make Trupeer where product knowledge lives

  • Living content: when a customer's UI changes, re-record one step and regenerate the video and guide.
  • Recurring jobs as templates: release-update videos, onboarding series, SOP libraries.
  • Knowledge hub as the default destination, so content builds up in one searchable place.
Experiment: concierge "update one step" for 50 accounts with 3+ guides vs a control group; measure 60-day retention. Target: accounts creating in 3 of their first 8 weeks.
B · Invisible ROI, single-player

Make value visible and shared

  • Viewer analytics and a monthly ROI digest (views, completion, top content, tickets deflected) for the admin and buyer.
  • Invite at the moment of sharing, plus comments, approvals and shared brand kits.
  • Enterprise: account health score, onboarding that reaches 2+ teams, quarterly reviews built on the digest.
Experiment: send the ROI digest to half of Scale accounts; measure renewal rate and seat expansion.
C · AI rework

Shorten time to a first good video

  • Guided first video by use case (sales demo, how-to, training) with a short pre-recording checklist.
  • Quality feedback loop: thumbs up/down on voiceover, script and doc steps; track edit time and fix the top failure modes.
Experiment: A/B test guided onboarding; measure 2nd piece within 14 days and median edit time.
D · Commercial

Stop avoidable losses

  • Payment recovery: smart retries, card updater, reminders.
  • Cancel flow: capture the reason, then offer a 1–3 month pause, a downgrade or a minute top-up.
  • Packaging: test flexible minutes, an occasional-creator seat, and annual nudges after a moment of value.
  • Acquisition: shift spend away from channels whose cohorts churn fastest.
Experiment: add pause to the cancel flow; measure save rate and 90-day reactivation.
06 · Measure

How I'd know it's working

North star

Weekly active workspaces whose content was viewed

Workspaces that published at least one piece in the week and had at least one viewer. It captures both the creation habit and the value delivered to viewers, which is what the buyer pays for.

Input metrics

Account health score

Leading churn signals

  • No creation for 21+ days
  • Active editors or views falling
  • Plan limit hit, or minutes barely used
  • Payment failure
  • Negative support sentiment
  • Champion left the company (Enterprise)
Amber triggers an in-app nudge; red triggers a CS call.
Guardrails & targets

Don't fake success

  • Guardrails: AI cost and gross margin per account, support load, CSAT, and "saved" accounts that churn within 90 days anyway.
  • Day 30: baseline and tracking live.
  • Day 60: activation up 10 points.
  • Day 90: self-serve logo churn down 25–30% (relative); over half of failed payments recovered.
Targets are illustrative and get set after the baseline.

08 · Risks

Trade-offs and what I wouldn't do

The biggest risk is treating every cancel as a product failure when some users were never a fit.

Retaining the wrong users

If one channel drives most churn, the fix is acquisition, not features.

Discount-led saves

They only delay churn; tracked through the 90-day guardrail.

Living content is heavy

Start concierge-style; build only once retention moves.

Noisy small cohorts

Pair every number with interviews before deciding.

Rollover hurts margin

Model AI cost before changing packaging.

CS doesn't scale

Automate the health score and ROI digest first.

What I wouldn't do: ship more avatars and voices as a churn fix, force annual contracts on unhappy users, or hide the cancel button.

10 · Questions

What I'd ask the founders

  1. Where is churn concentrated today: self-serve logos, Scale downgrades or Enterprise renewals?
  2. What share of churned accounts created only one or two pieces of content?
  3. Do we see who views customers' content, and do retained accounts have more viewers?
  4. Which acquisition channels produce the longest-lived customers?
  5. What do customers say when they cancel, and is that captured anywhere today?
